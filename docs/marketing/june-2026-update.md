**Title:** We studied 61 websites to see which actually get cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude. Here's what predicts AI citations (and what doesn't).

We run GeoSource.ai, a GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) scoring tool. We wanted to know: does our score actually predict whether AI cites your content?

So we ran a three-phase study. 61 websites. 17 industries. 540+ citation checks across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude.

**What predicts AI citations:**

1. **Answerability** (+109% lift) — Content that directly answers questions without making you dig. Lead with the answer, use declarative statements.

2. **Citation Quality** (+65% lift) — Sites that cite authoritative external sources earn more AI citations themselves. AI trusts content that shows its work.

3. **Definitions** (+33% lift) — Explicit "X is Y" statements. AI needs unambiguous definitions to cite with confidence.

**What doesn't predict citations (despite what people assume):**

- **Structure** — Nearly all sites score well on headings and lists. It's table stakes.
- **Schema markup** — Helps AI parse content but doesn't independently drive citations.
- **E-E-A-T signals** — Important for trust but confounded by site type in our data.

**The insight for smaller sites:**

Informational content gets cited at dramatically higher rates than product pages. Healthcare content: 67% citation rate. Travel: 83%. SaaS product pages: 13%. Ecommerce: 7%.

If you're a small company competing with big brands, create educational content. "How to choose a CRM" gets cited more than "buy our CRM."

### GeoSource.ai Publishes First Empirical Study on What Predicts AI Search Citations

*Three-phase study of 61 websites reveals Answerability, Citation Quality, and Definitions as strongest predictors — and rebuilds scoring algorithm based on evidence*

**[City, June 2026]** — GeoSource.ai today published the results of a three-phase research study examining which content optimization factors predict whether AI search platforms cite websites. The study analyzed 61 websites across 17 industries with over 540 citation checks across ChatGPT, Perplexity AI, and Claude.

The research identified three content pillars that most strongly predict AI citations: Answerability (+109% citation lift), Citation Quality (+65%), and Definitions (+33%). The study also found that content type is the single strongest external factor, with informational content cited at 5x the rate of product pages.

"Most GEO tools score content based on theoretical assumptions about what AI platforms care about," said Matt Laszkiewicz, founder of GeoSource.ai. "We wanted to test those assumptions against real citation data. Some of what we found confirmed expectations — direct answers matter. Other findings challenged conventional wisdom — schema markup and content structure are baseline requirements, not differentiators."

Based on the findings, GeoSource.ai has rebalanced its 12-pillar scoring algorithm to weight pillars according to their empirically-measured correlation with AI citations. The company also introduced a Citation Readiness Score — a focused metric built from the three proven predictive pillars. In testing, sites with high Citation Readiness Scores were cited 55.6% of the time compared to 33.3% for low scorers, a 67% improvement.

The full study, including methodology, data visualizations, and downloadable findings, is available at geosource.ai/blog/geo-citation-study.
